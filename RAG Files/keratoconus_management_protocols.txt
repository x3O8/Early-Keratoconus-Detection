KERATOCONUS MANAGEMENT PROTOCOLS — CLINICAL DECISION GUIDE
============================================================

## SEVERITY-BASED MANAGEMENT ALGORITHM

### NORMAL / NO KERATOCONUS (Probability < 20%)
- No intervention required
- Annual monitoring if any risk factors present (family history, atopy, eye-rubbing)
- Counsel on risk factors; advise against eye-rubbing
- Standard refractive correction (spectacles/soft CLs)

### SUBCLINICAL / FORME FRUSTE (Probability 20-40%, Severity: Subclinical)
- Topographically normal or borderline
- Biannual tomography monitoring
- Slit-lamp for signs of Fleischer ring, Vogt's striae
- Genetic counselling if family history
- Strict eye-rubbing cessation
- Allergy management (antihistamines for atopy)
- Treat amblyopia if present
- ABSOLUTE contraindication to LASIK/refractive surgery

### MILD KERATOCONUS (Probability 40-65%, Kmax 47-50 D)
- Spectacles or soft toric CL for optical correction
- Rigid Gas Permeable (RGP) lenses if spectacle BCVA inadequate
- Tomography every 6 months; refraction every 6 months
- CXL if progression documented (Kmax increase >= 1 D/year, pachymetry decrease >= 10 μm/year)
- Minimum pachymetry criteria for CXL: >= 400 μm (standard), 350-400 μm (modified protocols)
- Age < 35 years: lower threshold for CXL given longer disease course

### MODERATE KERATOCONUS (Probability 65-85%, Kmax 50-55 D)
- RGP contact lenses as primary optical correction
- Scleral lenses if RGP intolerance (superior comfort, better vision)
- Hybrid lenses as alternative
- CXL strongly recommended if any progression or age < 35
- Consider ICRS (Intacs/Keraring) for CL intolerance or to regularise cornea pre-CXL
- Athens protocol (topo-PRK + CXL) if irregular astigmatism and adequate pachymetry
- Monitoring: 4-6 monthly topography

### SEVERE / ADVANCED KERATOCONUS (Probability > 85%, Kmax > 55 D)
- Scleral lenses: primary optical strategy, often best achievable BCVA
- CXL: halt progression even in advanced KC if pachymetry allows
- DALK (Deep Anterior Lamellar Keratoplasty): first-choice transplant
  - Preserves host endothelium, lower rejection risk than PKP
  - Indicated: CDVA < 6/18 despite best CL, Kmax > 60 D, CL intolerance, central scarring without endothelial involvement
- PKP (Penetrating Keratoplasty): full-thickness; reserved for DALK failure, significant endothelial involvement
- Topography-guided PRK + CXL: may avoid surgery in some advanced cases
- Monitoring: 3-4 monthly

## CORNEAL CROSS-LINKING (CXL) PROTOCOLS

### Standard Dresden Protocol:
- Epithelial debridement (epi-off), 0.1% riboflavin in 20% dextran q2min x 30 min
- UV-A 3 mW/cm² x 30 min (total fluence 5.4 J/cm²)
- Minimum pachymetry: 400 μm de-epithelialised

### Accelerated CXL:
- 9 mW/cm² x 10 min; 18 mW/cm² x 5 min; 30 mW/cm² x 3 min
- Same total fluence, equivalent long-term outcomes
- Faster patient treatment, preferred in many centres

### Trans-epithelial (Epi-on) CXL:
- Preserves epithelium; reduced pain, faster recovery
- Lower riboflavin penetration — less established, some studies show inferior efficacy
- May be considered in thin corneas as alternative to standard protocol

### CXL Outcomes:
- Halts progression in ~90% of cases at 5 years
- UCVA and BCVA often improve 1-2 lines within 12 months
- Kmax typically reduces 1-2 D by 12 months
- Haze in first 1-3 months: expected, resolves
- Demarcation line at ~300 μm depth: marker of treatment depth

## CONTACT LENS SELECTION GUIDE

### RGP (Rigid Gas Permeable):
- Indication: mild-moderate KC, regular corneal topography, adequate tear film
- Lens design: aspheric, reverse geometry, multicurve back surface
- Fitting: 3-point touch or apical clearance technique
- Dk (oxygen transmissibility): > 100 Barrer preferred

### Scleral Lenses (16-22 mm):
- Indication: moderate-advanced KC, RGP intolerance, irregular cornea
- Vault 200-400 μm over cornea; saline-filled reservoir
- Eliminates corneal bearing; superior comfort
- Consider when: central scarring, pellucid marginal degeneration coexistence

### Hybrid Lenses:
- RGP centre (5-6 mm) bonded to soft hydrogel skirt
- Better comfort than RGP, better optics than soft
- SynergEyes, UltraHealth FC designs

## PATIENT COUNSELLING POINTS

1. Keratoconus is NOT caused by wearing glasses or contact lenses
2. Eye rubbing is the most important modifiable risk factor — absolute cessation essential
3. Family members should be screened (first-degree relatives)
4. CXL does not restore vision — it halts progression; contact lenses continue to be needed
5. KC rarely leads to blindness with appropriate management
6. Corneal transplant (if needed) has > 90% 10-year graft survival in KC
7. Young patients (teens-20s) need more frequent monitoring as progression is faster
8. Allergy management is crucial — antihistamines, mast cell stabilisers for atopic disease
