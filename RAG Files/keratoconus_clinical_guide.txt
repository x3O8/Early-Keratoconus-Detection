KERATOCONUS CLINICAL GUIDE — COMPREHENSIVE REFERENCE
=====================================================

## DEFINITION AND EPIDEMIOLOGY

Keratoconus (KC) is a progressive, non-inflammatory ectatic corneal disorder characterised by stromal thinning
and conical protrusion of the cornea, resulting in irregular myopia and astigmatism. It affects approximately
1 in 375 to 1 in 2000 individuals in the general population, with onset typically in the second decade of life.
The condition is bilateral but frequently asymmetric.

Risk factors include: atopy, eye-rubbing, family history (8–10× increased risk in first-degree relatives),
Down's syndrome, connective tissue disorders (Marfan's, Ehlers-Danlos), and contact lens wear.

## PATHOPHYSIOLOGY

KC is associated with decreased keratocyte density, disruption of the regular lamellar arrangement of collagen
fibrils, and increased expression of degradative enzymes (matrix metalloproteinases, cathepsins). The weakened
stroma under normal intraocular pressure and biomechanical stress leads to progressive ectasia. Oxidative stress
and free-radical damage may play a role in stromal degradation.

Biomechanically, KC corneas show reduced corneal hysteresis (CH) and corneal resistance factor (CRF) on
Ocular Response Analyser (ORA), and abnormal Corvis ST parameters including reduced stiffness parameter A1.

## CLASSIFICATION AND STAGING

### Amsler-Krumeich Classification (classical):
- Stage I: Central irregular astigmatism, eccentric corneal steepening; Kmax < 48 D
- Stage II: Kmax 48–53 D; no central scarring; rigid contact lens (RCL) corrected VA ≥ 6/60
- Stage III: Kmax 53–55 D; hard contact lens corrected VA < 6/60; average central K > 53 D
- Stage IV: Kmax > 55 D; central scarring; contact lens corrected VA < 6/60; mean K > 55 D

### ABCD Grading System (Pentacam):
Grades anterior curvature (A), posterior elevation (B), minimum corneal thickness (C), and CDVA (D)
independently from 0 (no ectasia) to 4 (severe) for each parameter.

### Belin-Ambrosio Enhanced Ectasia Display (BAD-D):
- BAD-D ≤ 1.6: Normal
- BAD-D 1.6–2.6: Suspicious / forme fruste
- BAD-D > 2.6: Keratoconus likely

### Topographic Indices:
- Kmax (maximum keratometry): Normal < 47.2 D; KC suspect 47.2–50 D; KC > 50 D
- K-average (mean K): Normal < 45 D
- ISV (Index of Surface Variance): measures asymmetry; normal < 37
- IVA (Index of Vertical Asymmetry): normal < 0.28
- KI (Keratoconus Index): > 1.07 suggests KC
- CKI (Central Keratoconus Index): > 1.03 suggests KC
- IHA (Index of Height Asymmetry): > 19 suspicious

## CORNEAL TOMOGRAPHY FINDINGS

### Anterior Elevation Map (Elv_A):
Best-fit sphere (BFS) elevation: Normal ≤ +12 μm. KC shows characteristic "island" of anterior elevation
typically > +15–20 μm above BFS, inferiorly displaced in typical KC, centrally in central KC.
A focal anterior elevation > 15 μm at the thinnest point strongly suggests ectasia.

### Posterior Elevation Map (Elv_P):
More sensitive than anterior. Elevation > +15 μm above BFS considered suspicious.
KC typically shows posterior elevation > +20–30 μm. The posterior surface protrudes before the anterior
surface in early KC, making posterior elevation the most sensitive tomographic marker.

### Corneal Thickness (CT_A) Map:
Normal minimum pachymetry 500–580 μm. KC: minimum pachymetry often < 500 μm, frequently < 460 μm in
moderate-to-advanced disease. Progressive inferior thinning with the thinnest point displaced inferiorly.
Pachymetric progression index (PPI) and % thickness increase from thinnest point to periphery are elevated.

### Sagittal Curvature Maps (Sag_A, Sag_P):
Anterior sagittal (axial) map: characteristic inferior steepening "claw" pattern or central steepening.
Normal flat-to-steep ratio is symmetric; KC shows asymmetric bow-tie or skewed axes.
Sag_P: posterior sagittal curvature also steepened, consistent with posterior protrusion.

### Epithelial Curvature Maps (EC_A, EC_P):
Epithelium compensates for stromal irregularity — thin over the cone (negative epithelial thickness changes).
OCT-based epithelial mapping: thinning at the cone apex often precedes stromal changes.
"Doughnut" pattern (central thin zone) is pathognomonic for early keratoconus.

## EARLY / SUBCLINICAL KERATOCONUS (FORME FRUSTE)

Topographically normal fellow eye of confirmed KC patient. The most challenging diagnostic scenario.
Criteria for forme fruste KC (FFKC):
- Normal corneal topography (no classic inferior steepening)
- One or more of: posterior elevation > 13 μm, minimum pachymetry < 490 μm, epithelial thinning pattern
- Abnormal biomechanics (reduced CH, increased ABCD)
- Genetic predisposition (positive family history)

The Topographic Keratoconus Classification (TKC) scale by Rabinowitz and Rasheed is widely used:
- TKC 0: Normal
- TKC 1 / TKC 1-2: Forme fruste / suspicious
- TKC 2 / TKC 3: Mild-moderate KC
- TKC 4: Advanced KC

## DIAGNOSIS

### Corneal Topography (Placido disc):
First-line screening. Detects surface irregularity. Cannot measure posterior surface or full-thickness.

### Corneal Tomography (Scheimpflug imaging: Pentacam, Galilei):
Full-thickness pachymetry, anterior + posterior elevation, curvature maps, indices.
Gold standard for KC diagnosis and staging.

### Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT):
High-resolution corneal cross-section. Epithelial mapping. Bowman's layer integrity.

### Corneal Biomechanics:
ORA: Corneal Hysteresis < 9.4 mmHg and CRF < 9.6 mmHg suggest KC.
Corvis ST: Stiffness parameter A1 (SP-A1) < 75.0 N/m suggests biomechanical weakening.

### Wavefront / Aberrometry:
Irregular higher-order aberrations (coma, trefoil) quantified. Total coma > 0.3 μm at 6 mm zone suspicious.

## MANAGEMENT

### Observation:
Mild KC (Kmax < 50 D, pachymetry > 490 μm, stable refraction) with stable topography over 12–24 months.
Spectacle correction: effective in early KC.
Soft toric CLs: useful in mild KC.

### Corneal Cross-Linking (CXL):
Standard of care for progressive KC. Riboflavin-UVA photopolymerisation stiffens the stroma.
Indications: documented progression (Kmax increase ≥ 1D/year, pachymetry decrease ≥ 2%/year), age < 35,
pachymetry ≥ 400 μm at thinnest point (Dresden protocol).
Accelerated CXL (9 mW/cm² × 10 min): equivalent efficacy to standard Dresden (3 mW/cm² × 30 min).
Trans-epithelial (epi-on) CXL: avoids epithelial debridement, lower penetration depth, less established.
Success: halts progression in ~90% of cases.

### Contact Lens Fitting:
- Rigid Gas Permeable (RGP): mask irregular astigmatism; first-line for moderate KC
- Hybrid lenses: RGP centre with soft skirt; improved comfort
- Scleral lenses: 16–22 mm diameter; vault the cornea; for moderate-to-advanced KC; best BCVA
- Mini-scleral/corneo-scleral lenses: intermediate option
- Piggyback system: soft lens beneath RGP

### Surgical Options:
Intrastromal Corneal Ring Segments (ICRS / Intacs / Keraring):
- Flatten the cone by arc-shortening effect
- Suitable for mild-moderate KC (Kmax 45–55 D, CDVA < 6/9, pachymetry ≥ 450 μm)
- Often combined with CXL ("CXL plus")

Topography-guided Photorefractive Keratectomy (topo-PRK):
- Small ablation to regularise corneal surface, typically combined with accelerated CXL
- "Athens Protocol" / "TCRB" (topography-guided CXL combined with PRK)

Corneal Transplantation:
- Deep Anterior Lamellar Keratoplasty (DALK): replaces stroma + epithelium, preserves endothelium; preferred
- Penetrating Keratoplasty (PKP): full-thickness; reserved for failed DALK, significant scarring, poor DALK candidate
- Indications: contact lens intolerance, CDVA < 6/18 despite best correction, Kmax > 60 D, central scarring

### Contraindications:
Refractive surgery (LASIK, PRK) is absolutely contraindicated in KC and FFKC — risk of post-LASIK ectasia.
CXL contraindicated if pachymetry < 400 μm (risk of endothelial damage), active herpetic keratitis, pregnancy.

## MONITORING

Frequency:
- Stable mild KC: 12-monthly topography + refraction
- Post-CXL: 1, 3, 6, 12 months, then annually
- Progressive KC: 4–6 monthly until stability confirmed over 2 consecutive visits

Parameters to Monitor:
- Kmax (maximum keratometry) — primary progression marker
- Thinnest pachymetry (mm location + value)
- BSCVA and BCVA
- Posterior elevation max (BFS)
- ISV, IVA, KI indices
- BAD-D score (Pentacam Enhanced Ectasia)
- Corneal hysteresis and CRF if ORA available

Progression Criteria (trigger for intervention):
- Kmax increase ≥ 1.0 D in 12 months
- Minimum pachymetry decrease ≥ 2% (or ≥ 10 μm) in 12 months
- BSCVA decrease of ≥ 2 Snellen lines
- Manifest refraction spherical equivalent change > 0.5 D/year

## DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS

- Pellucid marginal degeneration (PMD): inferior peripheral thinning, "butterfly" or "crab-claw" topographic pattern
- Post-LASIK ectasia: iatrogenic ectasia following refractive surgery; history key
- Keratoglobus: diffuse thinning of entire cornea; globe-like protrusion
- Posterior keratoconus: localised posterior thinning, stable, non-progressive
- Contact lens warpage: reverses after CL cessation; topography normalises

## GENETICS

Autosomal dominant with incomplete penetrance and variable expressivity most commonly described.
Candidate genes: VSX1, COL4A3/4, DOCK9, FNDC3B, IL1B, TGFBI, LOX (lysyl oxidase — key for collagen cross-linking).
Genetic testing not routine in clinical practice but helpful in family screening.
